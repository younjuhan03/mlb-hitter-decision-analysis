# Retained decision follow-up rules

These rules are transcribed from Appendix E of the supplied English PDF on 2026-10-04. The uploaded ZIP did not contain the earlier addendum or its claimed hash file. This transcription does not reconstruct an original registration or amend the frozen 2027 forecasts and evaluation protocol.

- Keep M1 as the default forecast and allow at most five exploratory signal investigations each year. Four or five successes in 2027 alone do not establish general superiority.
- First cumulative review: 2027-2029, at most 15 slots. If neither the pilot nor stopping conditions apply, continue unchanged through 2031, at most 25 slots.
- Annual freeze: same variables, alpha=10, screen, and outcome definitions. Refit M1 using only completed outcomes. Preserve inputs, candidates, selected IDs, and comparator selections before each season. All comparators use the same annual k.
- Limited pilot adoption requires all three: screen growth rate at least 10 percentage points above M1; random one-sided upper-tail probability at most .025; PA availability no more than 10 percentage points below M1. Keep a budget of five hitters.
- If growth rate or PA availability is at least 10 percentage points below M1, stop using the screen as a priority filter and retain it for individual diagnosis. This does not establish general inferiority.
- If results remain inconclusive after 2029, continue unchanged. If still inconclusive in 2031, keep M1 and do not adopt priority filtering. Do not extend the review until favorable results appear.

The .025 threshold applies at each of the two cumulative reviews; no extra annual adoption tests apply. This is a one-sided upper tail P(random successes >= observed), distinct from the probability-ordered two-sided tail in the historical benchmark table. The 10-point margins are analyst-specified operating judgments, not optimized thresholds or cost estimates. These rules do not establish statistical superiority over M1. Usable-bat outcomes cannot substitute for growth criteria.

The binomial illustration assumes independent slots, null success rate .24 and alternative .40. Required success counts are 4/5, 8/15, and 11/25; powers are 8.7%, 21.3%, and 41.4%. The actual benchmark must instead use each future season's finite candidate pool and success count. Repeated hitters limit inference.
